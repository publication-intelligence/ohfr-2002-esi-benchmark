# Source-first cross-chapter checklist

All 425 supplied main-text pages are covered exactly once across 17 approved chunks by three candidate-unexposed source-first reviewers. Their 446 finding groups are retrieval prompts, not a benchmark size target. This consolidation does not approve a benchmark, confirm omissions from an unseen draft, or claim item-complete review.

The lead directly read pages 297–425 and inspected the other reviewers’ complete paraphrased ledgers and coverage attestations. Five pending visual chronology checks are now complete. Source-only input hashes match; existing notes and attestations are unchanged.

The JSON companion contains exact finding IDs, source pages, immutable input hashes, team coverage and every original uncertainty/qualification. Each row below asks whether the eventual completed draft preserves a useful route, either independently or within a clearly differentiated consolidated treatment.

Free worship, public support, majority religion, state church and exclusive established religion are different arrangements. |
| ISR-WR-013 | How did promises of regeneration affect schooling, professional standards, poor relief and hospitals? | Promises and rights differ from funded provision; property, teachers, nursing orders, endowments and local initiative supply separate causal routes. | ISR-WR-017 | How did crowds, sections, societies, guards and communes influence national politics, and why did their power decline? | Crowd, National Guard, Commune, section, revolutionary army and regular army are not interchangeable. | ISR-WR-018 | How did different forms of repression arise, whom did they affect, and how was responsibility later reassigned? | Judicial execution, massacre, civil-war killing, custodial mortality, religious coercion, Great Terror, White Terror and Directorial repression have different procedures and denominators. |
| ISR-WR-019 | How did rural resistance, Federalist urban revolt and later counter-revolution differ in aims and organization? | Anti-revolution is not always restoration; Federalism was neither uniformly royalist nor a plan to dismember France. Town/country, religious, economic and local autonomy grievances interact. |
| ISR-WR-020 | Why did Bourbon leaders, constitutional royalists, local insurgents and foreign allies repeatedly fail to act together? | Pures, constitutionals, princes, officers, peasants and foreign courts had different objectives; claimed authority/support differed from actual strength. | ISR-WR-021 | How did revolutionary mobilization transform military effectiveness, careers, domestic coercion and civilian burdens? | Technical continuity and revolutionary enthusiasm both matter; volunteer, requisition and annual-class systems differ. Tactical success, military extraction and political guardianship are distinct. |
| ISR-WR-022 | Why did war begin, radicalize the Revolution and repeatedly continue despite negotiations? | Court rescue hopes, generals’ authority, revolutionary export, strategic rivalry, colonial aims and Eastern priorities differ. Threats, perceptions, actual intentions and outcomes must not be conflated. |
| ISR-WR-023 | How did foreign reformers adapt French ideas without necessarily pursuing French objectives? | Belgian inherited liberties, Polish executive strengthening, British reform, Irish independence and French reconstruction are not equivalent. Educated leaders, peasants and French strategists could want incompatible ends. |
| ISR-WR-024 | How did promised liberation become extraction and client rule, and how did occupied regions respond differently? | Annexed territory, occupation, proposed republic and established sister state differ. Local versus imposed constitutions, elections versus purges, unity aspirations versus French strategy, and urban versus rural responses differ. |
| ISR-WR-025 | Why did the Directory’s search for a republican middle end in repeated purges and military dependence? | Constitutional design, Two Thirds Law, Fructidor, Floréal, Prairial1799 and Brumaire differ in agents and direction. | ISR-WR-026 | How did Bonaparte secure stability through war, property guarantees, administration and religious settlement while curtailing representation? | Sieyès’s proposed checks differed from Bonaparte’s executive ambitions. Official claims of completed revolution, popular relief, manipulated consent and sacrificed principles are separate. |
| ISR-WR-027 | Which economic effects were transitory, structural, beneficial or destructive, and for whom? | Maritime versus continental markets, silk versus cotton/war industries, income groups, regions and short-term collapse versus delayed recovery differ. Removal of constraints does not itself demonstrate entrepreneurial dynamism. |
| ISR-WR-028 | How did French national mobilization provoke rival nationalisms and changes in state power? | Universal rights and national superiority coexisted; old patriotism differs from new national-unity projects. Mass armies required taxation, police and conscription beyond France. |
| ISR-WR-029 | How did liberty, equality, revolutionary action and conservative response acquire new meanings? | Rights and practice differ; liberty symbols, fraternity chronology, property-protecting equality, modern revolutionary models and distinct conservative responses need separate treatment. Conspiracy allegations are attributed and rebutted. |
