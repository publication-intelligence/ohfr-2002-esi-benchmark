# Source-first review map: supplied pages 159–296

This phase precedes exposure to the completed successor draft. The six ledgers record 159 finding clusters with stable local IDs and separate reader-access routes. They are a source-derived comparison aid, not a target subject count or a finding that any actual draft omits these subjects. All 138 owned pages of CHUNK-007 through CHUNK-012 were read. No candidate index, candidate result, source synthesis proposal or historical benchmark conclusion was consulted.

The later draft review should preserve the following connected treatments while keeping the indicated distinctions retrievable:

- **International revolutionary analogy and divergence.** Belgian inherited liberties, Polish executive strengthening, British reform and French reconstruction share language without sharing objectives (IR007-003–015). The later Polish insurgency, French non-assistance and coalition distraction also require access beyond a general foreign-influence heading (IR009-003, 015–017).
- **War as a cause of political transformation.** Separate competing motives for war, diplomatic miscalculation, military organization, supply extraction, coalition weakness, and the domestic consequences of defeats (IR008-005–008, 013; IR009-002–014). French success combines revolutionary mobilization with technical continuity and opponents’ competing interests. Expansion, annexation and sister republics require access distinct from promised liberation (IR009-004–005, 018, 025, 027).
- **Institutions of popular action.** Sections, popular societies, the National Guard, fédérés, the Commune, revolutionary armies and watch committees performed different functions and changed over time (IR008-014–019; IR010-009–010, 025; IR011-017, 030–032). The successive mobilizations of June and August 1792, May–June and September 1793, Germinal and Prairial 1795 should not become one undifferentiated insurrection treatment.
- **Economic mechanisms and lived subsistence.** Colonial supply shocks, exchange depreciation, army purchasing, hoarding accusations, forced sales, formal price controls, wage controls, forced loans, currency emissions, cold and transport failure are distinct mechanisms (IR008-009–010; IR010-003–004; IR011-024–026; IR012-004, 015–016, 021, 024). Women’s action is substantive throughout, rather than a decorative participant category.
- **Religious division and policy changes.** Nonjuror conflict, deportation, religious motivation in western resistance, locally initiated dechristianization, formal protection of cults, the Supreme Being cult, separation of Church and State and restricted renewed worship need separate access (IR008-001, 004, 013, 019; IR010-005; IR011-019–023; IR012-005, 018–020). Constitutional clergy also suffered religious persecution; religious policy cannot be reduced to a struggle with refractory clergy alone.
- **Provincial revolt as several different phenomena.** Vendéan peasant rebellion, Breton guerrilla warfare and urban opposition to Jacobin government differ in origins, recruitment, aims and military form (IR010-005–008, 012–014, 019–024). Federalism was neither uniformly royalist nor an organized project to dismember the Republic. Workers and merchants could ally against local militants; reluctance to leave home undermined both urban departmental armies and western insurgents.
- **Faction labels and effective government.** The source explicitly rejects treating the Girondins as an organized party or governing ministry overthrown in June (IR010-017–018). Authority evolved through competing ministers, committees, emissaries, local bodies and later reporting chains. The Law of 14 Frimaire and subsequent centralization must retain implementation delays and resistance (IR011-001, 022–023; IR012-003, 006, 011).
- **Forms and distributions of repression.** September killings, provincial judicial terror, battlefield and civilian destruction in the Vendée, custodial mortality, dechristianization, centrally accelerated Paris trials and White Terror are related but not interchangeable (IR008-020; IR011-008–018; IR012-003, 014, 022–025). Estimates have different denominators. Named famous victims cannot substitute for the source’s account of ordinary victims and regional concentration.
- **Attribution, proof and uncertainty.** Burke, Paine and Barruel’s accounts are attributed positions. Real corruption does not prove the wider foreign-conspiracy allegations assembled around it (IR007-011–013; IR009-028; IR011-027–030). Danton’s fall has unusually weak motivational evidence. Robespierre’s dictatorship is explicitly rejected as an established description or proven ambition (IR012-002, 010). Thermidorian retrospective blame and Carrier’s prosecution raise shared-responsibility issues.
- **Thermidor as a contested transition.** The overthrow led to institutional dismantling, selective early continuities, street violence, religious change, deepening subsistence crisis, last popular risings and unresolved constitutional choices (IR012-011–026). Neither immediate consensus nor complete restoration accurately describes the sequence. Louis XVIII’s declaration then foreclosed important routes to a negotiated restoration.

## Source qualifications preserved

The ledgers retain qualified claims rather than silently strengthening them. Examples include southern peasant motives in 1792, coalition and insurgent calculations, proposed but incompletely implemented redistribution, social selectivity in the Great Terror, and speculation about committee tactics in Prairial.

Date/sequence flags concern the supplied source itself: the Convention’s opening and republican act on pp. 193–194; Cispadane creation and absorption dates on p. 215; Dumouriez’s defection on pp. 202 and 227; awkward Rennes chronology on p. 240; and Robespierre’s speech and subsequent sequence on pp. 279–280. The first, second and last groups were checked against PDF renders. No external chronology has been substituted.

Map 4 on p. 225 was inspected visually in full. Its eligible caption is recorded; geographic details are contextual and do not establish otherwise unwritten narrative treatments. No raw source text or images are included in these public artifacts.
